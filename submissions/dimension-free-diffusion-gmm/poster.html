Conditions kept visible

  • GMM components have covariance Id.
  • Component means obey the paper’s polynomial norm condition.
  • Score error is an average L² error over time.
  • The theorem uses TV distance; these checks use finite diagnostic proxies.

Evidence boundary

This synthetic CPU audit trains no score network and estimates no end-to-end TV distance. It does not establish the paper’s asymptotic complexity.

Independent identity

x′=(1−h)x+√(2h)z ⇒ Var(x′)=1+h²

The identity holds coordinate-wise. The empirical column is retained to expose finite-sample variation, while the claim decision uses the analytic range.

Scope verdict: toy support

The zero analytic range audits dimension independence in a single exact-score Gaussian step. It does not reproduce the theorem’s complete multi-step TV guarantee.

  • Threshold: analytic range ≤ 1e−12.
  • Observed range: exactly 0.
  • Dimensions: 1, 4, 16, 64.

The empirical deviations shrink irregularly with dimension because they are Monte Carlo diagnostics, not the analytic decision statistic.

Decision rule

The analytic mean shift must be nondecreasing with perturbation norm. Here it follows hε exactly at h=0.125, isolating the score-error term from sampling noise.

The empirical mean norm is logged as a diagnostic but is not substituted for the analytic controlled construction.

The paper’s theorem permits time-averaged L² score error; this one-step construction audits only the separability and monotonicity of a calibrated additive perturbation.

Misspecification control

Replacing the calibrated constant perturbation with a state-dependent cubic term violates the controlled-error setup. Its empirical second moment rises to 1.393.

  • Control: 0.5x³ state-dependent perturbation.
  • Observed empirical mean norm: 0.218.

Why dimension cancels

The component means occupy one fixed active coordinate. For unit covariance, the ambient −I term cancels inside tr(I+J), leaving the posterior covariance of component means.

tr(I+J)=tr(Cov[μₖ | x])

The implementation compares the analytic trace against centered finite differences at 2,048 deterministic evaluation points.

Unit-covariance control

Setting component variance to 0.05 breaks the unit-covariance setup and makes tr(I+J) strongly dimension-dependent. The baseline max range is 3.6e−15.

  • Baseline variance: 1.0.
  • Control variance: 0.05.
  • Control mean tr(I+J) changes from −9.0 at d=1 to −1206.0 at d=64.

The analytic trace is checked independently by centered finite differences.
